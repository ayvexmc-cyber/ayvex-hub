What makes you strong and look good

Every day covers protein, energy carbs, fiber, healthy fats, calcium, iron and vitamin C. Fish shows up a few times a week for omega-3.

Batch prep

Make these at once and most nights are just 5 minutes of putting things together.

Don't batch these: wraps and sandwiches (only the night before, or they go soggy), lunches with chicken, tuna or egg (2 days ahead at most), rice (1 day) and avocado (cut it fresh).

Your weekly routine

  1. Sunday: shop and prep about 30 min

    • Shop with that week's list. Put cold food in the fridge as soon as you get home.
    • Boil 6–8 eggs: cover them with cold water, bring to the boil, then simmer 10 min. Move them to cold water for 5 min. Keep them in the fridge in their shells.
    • Put your ice pack in the freezer. Keep it there every night.
    • Pull the meat off a rotisserie chicken (or open the cooked strips) into a tub. Fridge, use within 3–4 days.

  2. Wednesday: top up 5 min

    • Buy fresh cooked chicken for the rest of the week. The Sunday batch is done after 3–4 days.
    • Check the fridge. Throw out anything past its days in the fridge-rules list.

  3. Every night: pack tomorrow 10 min

    • After dinner, open tomorrow in the plan and make the packed lunch. It goes in a tub or foil, then the fridge.
    • If breakfast says "night before", make the overnight oats now.
    • Put leftovers in the fridge within 1 hour of cooking.

  4. Every morning: grab and go 2 min

    • Lunch from the fridge into an insulated lunch bag with the frozen ice pack on top.
    • Add the fruit and any snacks that don't need the fridge.

  5. After school or training within 1 hour

    • Eat the after-training snack. If it says "Fridge", pack it with the ice pack or eat it when you get home.

How long food keeps in the fridge

  • Cooked chicken, mince, pasta3–4 days
  • Cooked salmon or white fish2 days
  • Packed lunch with chicken, tuna or egg2 days
  • Overnight oats3 days
  • Boiled eggs, in the shell1 week
  • Opened ham or sliced turkey3–5 days
  • Opened hummus, cottage cheese5 days
  • Cooked rice1 day
  • Cut avocado1 day
  • Chili or curry, frozen3 months
Food that needs the fridge can't sit out for more than 2 hours (1 hour on a hot day). With an ice pack in an insulated bag, it's fine until lunch. Thaw prawns in the fridge or cold water, never on the counter. Reheat leftovers until steaming hot. If something smells off, throw it out.

Busy-guy shortcuts

Pack lunch at night

Make it after dinner, put it in the fridge, grab it in the morning.

Use an ice pack

An insulated bag with an ice pack keeps chicken, tuna, eggs and yogurt safe until lunch.

Buy chicken cooked

A rotisserie chicken or ready-cooked strips covers most lunches. No cooking.

Boil eggs in a batch

Boil 6 eggs for 10 min on Sunday. They keep 5 days in the fridge.

Frozen veg and rice pouches

No chopping and they don't go bad. Dinner in 15 minutes.

Eat after training

Have the after-training snack within an hour of practice. On really big days, add one more snack.

Enough protein

About 1 g per pound (2 g per kg) is all your muscles can use. More just gets burned as energy.

Sleep, water, training

Muscle grows with 9+ hours of sleep, lots of water and regular training. Food is one part.

Vitamin D

Food gives you little. Get outside in daylight, and ask a doctor if you're low in winter.
